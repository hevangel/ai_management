# Cover-Art Briefs

Per-publication cover-art briefs for the five-styles rewriter. Each cover visualizes a
different aspect of the argument — never one image recolored. Use the editorial design
logic rather than brand imitation: do not directly copy proprietary mastheads, logos, or
exact publication layouts unless the user explicitly asks for a parody or mockup and the
context permits it.

## Sloan Cover-Art Brief

**Visual objective:** communicate a new management concept.

**Style:** conceptual editorial illustration; flat or semi-flat; geometric;
intellectual; limited palette; clean negative space; human + system abstraction.

**Typical concept:** one human directs multiple abstract AI entities while human and
machine capacity converge into a shared organizational system.

**Image question:** "What is the new unit of organizational capacity?"

## HBR Cover-Art Brief

**Visual objective:** communicate one memorable managerial idea immediately.

**Style:** clever editorial metaphor; minimal composition; strong central object;
human-centered; visually understandable in one second.

**Typical concept:** a manager faces two doors — HIRE A PERSON versus ADD AI CAPACITY —
or an organizational chart where some "employees" are people and some are AI agents.

**Image question:** "Who — or what — should you hire next?"

## McKinsey Cover-Art Brief

**Visual objective:** show a future enterprise operating model.

**Style:** sophisticated; systemic; premium corporate; broad landscape; networked;
elegant; transformation-oriented.

**Typical concept:** a small group of human leaders stands above an expanding network of
machine agents, workflows, and decision nodes — humans as control and judgment points
within a much larger artificial-capacity system.

**Image question:** "What does the AI-native enterprise look like?"

## BCG Cover-Art Brief

**Visual objective:** show a strategic divergence between competitors.

**Style:** strategic diagram or visual system; curves; archetypes; contrasting
trajectories; competitive tension; data-like but editorial.

**Typical concept:** two companies begin at the same point — traditional company
(revenue ↑, headcount ↑↑) versus AI-native company (revenue ↑↑↑, headcount ↑ modestly,
AI capacity ↑↑↑) — their trajectories visibly diverging.

**Image question:** "How does AI change the competitive cost curve?"

## Bain Cover-Art Brief

**Visual objective:** show the capital-allocation decision.

**Style:** clean; high contrast; strong economic metaphor; simple central object;
decision-oriented.

**Typical concept:** a capital-allocation dial or balance — HUMANS ←→ AI CAPACITY — with
a third optimized zone, HUMAN + AI; or "NEXT $250K" with alternative allocations
beneath it.

**Image question:** "Where should the next dollar go?"

---

# Cover-Art Output Format

After each article, provide:

### Cover concept

One paragraph describing the visual.

### Key metaphor

One sentence.

### Composition

Foreground, background, visual hierarchy, and subject placement.

### Art direction

Medium; geometry; visual density; realism; palette direction; typography approach if
text appears.

### Avoid

Elements that would make the visual generic or inconsistent with that editorial style.

### Image-generation prompt

A production-ready image prompt. Do not include proprietary logos or publication
mastheads unless explicitly requested.
