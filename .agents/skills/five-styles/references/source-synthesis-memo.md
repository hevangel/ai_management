# Source Synthesis Memo — Section Spec

Stage 0 of the five-styles rewriter. One page (600–1,000 words), written before any
article. The memo is the canonical source of truth for all later outputs — the five
articles and the cover-art briefs — so do not optimize it for any publication style.
When the author's thinking changes, revise this memo first, then regenerate only the
affected versions (SKILL.md → Revision Workflow).

## Working Thesis

State the strongest version of the argument in 1–3 sentences. It should be specific
enough to be falsifiable.

Avoid: "AI is changing management."

Prefer: "AI weakens the historical link between organizational growth and headcount
growth, forcing firms to allocate human labor and machine cognition as partially
substitutable forms of productive capacity."

## Conventional View

Describe the established worldview, assumption, or management practice being challenged.

## What Changed

Identify the technological, economic, organizational, regulatory, or competitive shift
that makes the conventional view less reliable.

## Causal Chain

Express the logic in 3–7 steps. Example:

AI reduces the marginal cost of cognitive execution
→ some work scales without proportional hiring
→ output and employment become less tightly coupled
→ headcount becomes an incomplete measure of organizational capacity
→ firms need human-AI capacity allocation.

## Core Mechanism

Explain why the thesis should be true. Distinguish mechanism from correlation, analogy,
or speculation.

## Key Concepts

Capture useful terminology, frameworks, equations, metaphors, and phrases — e.g.
human-AI production function; span of agency; headcount-led growth; capacity-led growth;
complementarity; substitution; saturation; VC-style growth + PE-style operating leverage.
Preserve strong original terminology from the source.

## Evidence

Separate evidence into:

- **Empirical** — research, quantitative findings, measured outcomes.
- **Business examples** — companies, industries, organizational cases.
- **Practitioner** — direct experience or observations contained in the source.
- **Logical** — arguments derived from first principles.

## Strongest Counterarguments

Include serious objections, not straw men.

## Unresolved Questions

Preserve uncertainty. Do not invent answers merely to make the article cleaner.

## Managerial Implications

State 3–7 implications that follow if the thesis is correct.

## Claims Requiring Verification

Identify current, factual, numerical, company-specific, legal, scientific, or research
claims requiring external verification before publication.

## Authorial Position

When the source is conversational, summarize the author's most developed position,
distinguishing between: early speculation; ideas later rejected; refined positions;
unresolved beliefs.

## One-Sentence Version

Finish with one sentence explaining the argument to a sophisticated executive.
