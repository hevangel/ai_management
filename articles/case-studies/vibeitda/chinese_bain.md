# 你的下一個美元應該花在人身上還是 AI 上？

Companies are spending rapidly on AI, but many still budget for it incorrectly。

AI sits inside the technology budget。

People sit inside the workforce budget。

Managers negotiate for headcount。

CIOs negotiate for software and infrastructure。

Finance evaluates the two largely separately。

That structure made sense when software primarily helped employees perform their jobs。

It becomes less useful when AI can perform portions of the work itself。

The economic question is increasingly straightforward：

**Should the next dollar of capacity come from another employee or from more AI？**

Most companies cannot answer it。

They should learn quickly。

## 新的邊際投資決定

Consider an engineering leader with an additional $250,000 of annual budget。

There are several choices。

Hire another engineer。

Give existing engineers significantly more agent capacity。

Hire a senior technical lead and pair that person with AI。

Invest in testing or infrastructure because code generation is no longer the bottleneck。

The right answer depends on marginal economics。

At a high level，leaders should compare：

**Return on additional human capacity**

with

**Return on additional AI capacity**

and, most importantly,

**Return on the combined human-AI system。**

The third category will often dominate。

The biggest mistake is to assume that AI is either purely complementary or purely substitutive。

It can be both。

## AI economics 的三個 zones

Every workflow will eventually reveal three economic zones。

### Zone 1: Complementarity

Additional AI increases the productivity of existing employees significantly。

Suppose an engineer costing $250,000 can normally generate $600,000 of economic value。

Providing $25,000 of AI capacity increases that value to $900,000。

That is a strong investment。

Removing the engineer would destroy much of the value because the AI depends on their judgment。

### Zone 2: Substitution

At some point, AI may allow the company to produce the same output with fewer people。

For example, a service team that once required 100 employees may need only 75 after processes are redesigned。

The relevant value is not "jobs automated."

It is：

**economic output maintained or improved at lower total cost。**

### Zone 3: Saturation

Eventually, more AI stops producing meaningful returns。

The team may generate more code than it can review。

More leads than salespeople can follow。

More analysis than executives can process。

At this point, the bottleneck lies elsewhere。

Additional token spending is waste。

The objective is to determine where each business process sits on this curve。

## 為什麼固定的 token budget 在經濟上是錯誤的

Many companies initially give employees equal or nearly equal AI limits。

That works during adoption。

It is not an efficient long-term system。

Suppose Employee A generates $10 of incremental value for every $1 of additional AI capacity。

Employee B generates $2。

Employee C generates almost no incremental value because their workflow is constrained elsewhere。

A flat budget systematically underfunds A and overfunds C。

Companies would never allocate investment capital this way。

They should not allocate AI capacity this way either。

The correct principle is simple：

**Fund AI to the point where its marginal return falls below the next-best use of capital。**

That sounds obvious。

In practice, almost no workforce-management system is designed to do it。

## 從 usage 開始，而非 economics

AI programs often track：

- active users；
- prompts；
- tokens；
- agent sessions；
- generated code；
- hours saved。

These metrics can help diagnose adoption。

They do not measure value。

An organization can save 100,000 employee hours and produce almost no financial benefit if employees use the saved time on low-value activities。

The relevant measures are closer to：

- incremental revenue；
- incremental gross profit；
- reduced cost to serve；
- shorter cycle time；
- increased customer retention；
- increased engineering throughput；
- improved asset utilization；
- avoided hiring。

The phrase "hours saved" should therefore trigger a second question：

**What happened to the hours?**

If nothing economically meaningful changed, there may be no value to capture。

## 將 AI productivity 轉化為 P&L outcomes

A useful AI initiative should ultimately connect to one of four financial mechanisms。

### Revenue growth

AI enables more customers, better conversion, faster product releases, more experimentation, or new products。

### Cost reduction

AI reduces labor, external services, rework, or process cost。

### Asset efficiency

AI enables the company to generate more output from existing people, systems, and capital。

### Risk reduction

AI improves detection, quality, compliance, or decision accuracy enough to reduce expected loss。

Every initiative should have a hypothesis linking AI adoption to one or more of these outcomes。

Otherwise it is technology spending, not transformation。

## 關鍵指標：邊際 capacity ROI

The useful management question is not：

"How productive is AI?"

It is：

**What is the ROI of the next unit of AI capacity relative to the next unit of human capacity?**

Imagine a product organization evaluating the next $100,000。

Option A: add 0.4 fully loaded employee equivalents。

Option B: increase agent capacity significantly。

Option C: invest in customer research。

Option D: improve testing infrastructure。

Management should compare the marginal business value created by each。

The answer may differ by team。

A team bottlenecked by implementation may have very high AI returns。

A team bottlenecked by product judgment may have low AI returns and high returns from hiring an experienced product leader。

This is why company-wide replacement ratios are misleading。

The relevant economics exist at the workflow level。

## 隱藏的 constraint：management quality

AI also changes the economic value of individual employees。

Some people will produce much greater returns from AI than others。

The reason is not necessarily technical skill。

The highest-leverage employees may excel at：

- framing the problem correctly；
- decomposing work；
- deciding what should not be automated；
- detecting bad outputs；
- coordinating multiple agents；
- integrating results into real systems；
- taking responsibility for the outcome。

These employees can productively deploy more artificial capacity。

That creates a new scarce asset：

**AI management capability。**

A company may discover that giving its best employees significantly more AI resources creates more value than distributing resources evenly。

The economic logic resembles capital allocation。

You fund the operators who produce the highest return。

## 這如何影響 headcount

This framework inevitably changes workforce planning。

Historically, business growth produced a headcount plan。

In the future, business growth should first produce a **capacity plan**。

The sequence becomes：

1. Define the desired outcome。
2. Identify the constraint。
3. Determine whether the constraint requires human judgment, AI execution, or another resource。
4. Compare marginal ROI across alternatives。
5. Add headcount only when it produces the highest return。

This does not imply a policy of minimizing employees。

In many cases, the best answer will be to hire。

A highly capable human combined with AI may create much more value than either resource alone。

The goal is not fewer people。

The goal is better economics。

## PE economics meet VC economics

This creates an unusual possibility。

Traditionally, companies pursuing very rapid growth often accepted weak near-term margins。

Companies pursuing maximum margins often sacrificed growth。

AI may allow companies to weaken this trade-off。

If revenue increases much faster than headcount, a business can produce both：

**rapid growth**

and

**operating leverage。**

In other words：

**VC growth economics and PE efficiency economics begin to converge。**

This should attract the attention of every CEO, CFO, and investor。

The most valuable AI-native businesses may not simply automate the most jobs。

They may be the companies that achieve the largest gap between output growth and labor growth without degrading customer outcomes。

## 五個問題 every business unit 應該回答

Before approving significant AI spending or additional hiring, leaders should ask：

**1. What business output are we trying to increase?**

Not activity. Output.

**2. What is currently constraining that output?**

Do not assume it is labor.

**3. What happens if we add another dollar of AI capacity?**

Measure the actual incremental result.

**4. What happens if we add another dollar of human capacity?**

Use the same economic standard.

**5. At what point does another resource become the bottleneck?**

This determines when to stop spending.

The result is not a static workforce model。

It is a continuous capital-allocation system。

## AI management 的下一個前沿

The first phase of enterprise AI was about access。

The second was about adoption。

The next phase will be about economics。

Companies will have to determine where AI complements labor, where it substitutes for labor, and where additional spending produces little value。

No universal exchange rate exists between salaries and tokens。

That is precisely the opportunity。

The companies that measure the relationship earlier will allocate resources better than companies relying on fixed headcount assumptions or flat AI allowances。

The old management question was：

**How many people should we hire?**

The better question is becoming：

**What is the highest-return mix of human and AI capacity for this outcome?**

That is not fundamentally a technology question。

It is a capital-allocation question。
